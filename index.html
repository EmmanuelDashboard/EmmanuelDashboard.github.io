    How to use

    1. Open an image. Drag a drone image of a pecan orchard onto the map (orthomosaic GeoTIFF, JPG or PNG), or click Open image or results. Drop its elevation (DSM) file together with it (or add it in the window that opens): tree heights take out grass and weeds and find every treetop. The canopy, crowns, planting grid and EVEN / ODD diagonals are found in your browser; nothing is uploaded. A JPG or PNG has no coordinates, so you enter the ground size of one pixel (no DSM then).
    2. Same results on Kaggle: the pecan canopy notebook runs this dashboard's engine, so the same image and DSM give exactly the same crowns and canopy there; drop its Pecan_Canopy_Results_….zip here to open them.
    3. Check. Each crown is tinted in the colour of its diagonal (blue EVEN, red ODD). Along every row the crowns alternate blue, red, blue, also across a missing tree. Quality checks confirms it (0 neighbours with the same colour) and Show takes you to any that are left. The number at the end of each line is the total canopy of that diagonal.
    4. Decide. Thinning plan: choose EVEN or ODD to see the trees and canopy removed and left, the canopy cover before and after, and the tree heights. Download trees to remove gives the crew a list with the GPS position of every tree to cut.
    5. Correct. Click a crown, then:
      • ‹ › moves it to the neighbouring diagonal (its colour changes);
      • Not a pecan takes it out of the totals;
      • Merge with… then click a second crown: two pieces of one tree become one crown;
      • Add missed crown: click around the crown on the map, double-click to finish.
      Ctrl+Z undoes, Esc cancels. Corrections are kept in this browser until you reset them.
    6. Where to start. Thinning removes the family (EVEN or ODD) with the smaller total canopy and starts on its first line: D01 when ODD is smaller, D02 when EVEN is smaller. You can choose another line; the thinning plan follows it. Start only shows just that line.
    7. Export the corrected crowns and the canopy per diagonal (EVEN and ODD totals at the end).

    About this dashboard

    Plan the diagonal thinning of a pecan orchard from one drone flight. Diagonal thinning removes every other tree in each row and column, so every tree belongs to one of two families: EVEN or ODD.

    What you get

    • Pecan canopy only: no grass, weeds, soil or shadow.
    • One crown per tree, tinted blue (EVEN) or red (ODD), young replanted trees included. Along every row the colours alternate, also across a missing tree.
    • Canopy of every diagonal, and the EVEN, ODD and total canopy, in square feet (ft²).
    • Where to start: the first line of the family with the smaller canopy.
    • A thinning plan and the list of trees to remove with their GPS positions, for the field crew.

    How it works

    • The canopy is found from the colour and texture of the image.
    • With the elevation (DSM) file, tree height = DSM − ground: only canopy at least 3 ft (1 m) tall counts, and clear cast shadow is trimmed from the crown edges.
    • The planting grid (20 × 40 ft design) is measured from the treetops, and the diagonals follow it.
    • Young trees are too small and too low for those rules, so a small, round, sunlit crown as green as the pecan leaves that stands on an empty planting position of a row is counted as a young tree (flag YOUNG_TREE). Every empty position of the 40 × 20 ft planting grid is also searched for a sapling: a spot of leaves on the tree strip, clearly greener than the ground around it. These saplings count only when the measured grid matches the design and the same search finds far fewer lone green spots halfway between planting positions, where nothing was planted. So grass, weeds and shadow are not counted. A line of young trees halfway between the positions (a row planted half a spacing off) is counted too.
    • A crown you add is measured without the parts other crowns already cover.
